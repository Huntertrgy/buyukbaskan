Stakeholders

A chairman answers to more than the scoreboard, and the Chairman Power screen shows your indicators and six stakeholder relationships with the reason behind every move.

Back to Wiki

The rule that never bends

No relationship ever changes a match result, a referee decision, a fine or a fine reduction. Good relations open doors; they never buy results or votes.

How relationships work

Each relationship runs from 0 to 100, and 50 is neutral. The bands read Hostile, Cold, Neutral, Warm and Allied.

A relationship moves with what you do, and every move records its reason: results (derbies and cup shocks count extra), your ticket policy, stadium and stand naming deals, sponsor deals, stand construction, facility investment, sacking or appointing a manager, how you answer the fans, debt warnings and how the season ends. Over time, relationships also drift.

The six stakeholders and what they do

  • Terraces, the supporters' association: watches ticket prices, the stadium name and stand investment. Home attendance moves by at most 3% either way, never above capacity.
  • Press, the local sports paper: writes about results, your statements and manager decisions. At 75 or above, fan approval gains 1 every 4 weeks; at 25 or below it loses 1. Nothing else.
  • Pundits, the local TV football panel: judges your manager and your decisions. At 75 or above, trust in the manager gains 1 every 4 weeks; at 25 or below it loses 1. Nothing else.
  • City hall, the municipality and city administration: follows stadium and facility investment, cheap tickets and debt. Stand construction cost moves by at most 5%.
  • Business, the local business association: wants sponsorship, naming rights and financial discipline. Sponsor and naming offers move by at most 5%.
  • Federation, the federation's regional office: notes youth work, facilities and financial order. No effect.
  • The fans relationship and the average of city hall and business also count in the members' vote at the General Assembly.

The fans and the manager

Besides the relationships, Chairman Power tracks four indicators: fan approval, trust in the manager, manager happiness and board trust.

  • Wins and derby results move fan approval most. Selling the stadium name costs 40 points.
  • Promising patience or improvement when the fans ask, investing in training or youth and opening a new stand all lift approval.
  • The manager is pleased when you approve his transfer requests, fix squad and pitch complaints and invest in training and youth. Every refused request lowers his happiness.

Political connections

Now and then a familiar contact from city hall or the business circle offers money, or to cover part of the next facility step. Accepting spends some of your goodwill with them, the press may notice the closeness, and a favour will be asked back a few weeks later. Turning that favour down cools the relationship. You can always politely decline the offer.

Club incidents

Some weeks bring a club incident with a few options. Before you choose, the card shows each option's price; the result post afterwards tells you what changed. None of them changes a match, a referee or a fine.
