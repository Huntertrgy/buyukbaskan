Finance

Wages, transfers and building work all come out of one bank account, and the Finances screen shows where it stands.

Back to Wiki

Where the money comes from

The Finances screen shows your balance, wage bill, ticket price and transfer budget, the income and expense ledger, sponsors, history and a cash flow forecast. Settings such as the ticket price change through decisions in the news.

  • Matchday income: ticket income, food and drink, the shop, parking and the VIP lounge (see Match Day).
  • Season tickets: a campaign (value, balanced or premium) is offered in the news after the pre-season friendly and can be launched until the first home league match. The cash comes up front and is not refunded. It is a bet: it gains in a bad season and loses when the ground would fill anyway.
  • Sponsors: the shirt sponsor you choose from the offers, stadium naming rights, and stand naming sponsors in the professional leagues. The VIP lounge, the museum and stand sponsors together add at most 25% to sponsor income. Selling the stadium name costs fan approval.
  • Prize money and player sales.

Raise Funds

The Raise Funds screen opens from the Funds tile on the home screen or from Finances. It offers rewarded videos (up to 10 a season, the reward grows with the division), three backing packs (supporter, business, patron) and the Super Stadium through Google Play Billing.

Rewarded videos and purchases work in the Android app only. A paid purchase that was not added yet is finished automatically when the game opens or when you open Finances or Raise Funds; you can also use Settings > Restore purchases.

  • Backing packs: a fixed amount for the bank, and the fans and the manager trust the chairman more.
  • Super Stadium: built at once for the current club; it can also be bought with in-game money.
  • Rewarded video: a reward set by the club's division, at most 10 a season.
  • In-game copy can frame offers as municipal support, government aid, supporter donations, or sponsor investment.

Where it goes

  • Wages and win bonuses: the board sends a wage budget recommendation, and the players ask for win, season target and cup bonuses as a percentage of their wages.
  • Stadium and running costs, in three parts: a fixed running charge set by your division and the fans' expectations, stand upkeep that grows with capacity, and 15% of the gate at every home match.
  • Facility upkeep for open outlets, modules and the medical centre, plus backroom staff wages.
  • Transfers: fees, agent commission and signing bonuses.

The transfer budget

Each season the financial director asks you to set the transfer budget. The manager reacts to your choice, and the fans dislike cuts. Each window's spending limit applies on top (see Transfers and Contracts).

Debt and the overdraft

  • The club can go below zero down to its overdraft limit. The bank charges interest while the account is overdrawn, and promotion raises the limit.
  • Warnings arrive when 75% of the credit limit is in use and when you get close to it.
  • Above the limit, a recovery window starts and a bankruptcy warning tells you how many finance reports are left. Sell players or cut wages.
  • Trustee rescue: your first bankruptcy does not end the career. A trustee sells up to 5 of your players, 6 league points are deducted, and you cannot sign players for one transfer window. This happens once per career; a second bankruptcy ends it.

End of the year

The year closes with an annual profit or loss. Profit is taxed; investment the club received through backing is not. Sometimes a tax rebate arrives instead.
